Independent acceleration derivative investigation

Sources and symbols: data/train.csv (all 3522 rows, all 13 trajectories) fits coefficients; data/val.csv (all 1192 rows, all 3 trajectories) assesses errors only. `y`, `x1` are logged states, `x2` is logged input, and `dx1` is derivative target. Physical units are not supplied. All RMSE and residual values below have the unknown `dx1` unit. Coefficient units are derivative-unit divided by associated feature-unit. `w` has inverse-y-unit; `s` has x1-unit.

Methods and settings: ordinary least squares using numpy.linalg.lstsq after column norm scaling, jointly estimating coefficients without starting values or randomness. Criterion: training mean squared derivative error. RMSE means `sqrt(mean((dx1-prediction)**2))`. Families tried: linear and odd-polynomial restoring; sine restoring with frequency search; harmonics; velocity-sign and smooth saturated friction; nonlinear state/input interactions; training-selected forward term inclusion. Frequency search uses 160 grid points over `0.1 <= w <= 4.0` inverse-y-unit and bounded scipy.optimize.minimize_scalar refinement, tolerance `1e-10` inverse-y-unit. Smooth friction scales are fixed sensitivity settings. These are mathematical hypotheses, not known physical mechanisms. Reference from memory, not externally checked: Golub and Van Loan, Matrix Computations (least squares).

Residual patterns and nonlinear terms
Sine-baseline residual `dx1-prediction` has correlation with `sign(x1)` of -0.44623 on training and -0.36883 on validation (dimensionless). Training mean residual is +14.53121 unknown dx1-unit for `-4.0841 <= x1 < 0` unknown x1-unit, versus -12.86951 unknown dx1-unit for `0.47124 <= x1 < 3.927` unknown x1-unit. This systematic antisymmetric velocity dependence motivates friction. Baseline lag-one residual correlation is 0.821785 training / 0.569401 validation (dimensionless; lag 0.01 s). Full-basis validation lag-one correlation remains 0.414017 (dimensionless), so residuals are not white.

Adding one term to sine baseline gives validation RMSE: `x1*Abs(x1)` 17.39554, `x1**3` 17.79059, `x1*y` 18.24171, `x1*sin(w*y)` 18.26405, `x2**3` 18.43837, `x2*Abs(x2)` 18.44591, `x2*y**2` 18.43849, `x2*cos(w*y)` 18.45199, all unknown dx1-unit on all validation rows. State-dependent damping provides small improvements; tested input-only nonlinearities do not improve baseline validation. Full-model validation residual mean remains +7.480874 unknown dx1-unit for `-3.7699 <= x1 < -0.15708` unknown x1-unit; moderate positive velocities have high residual RMSE, 27.10272 unknown dx1-unit for `0.62832 <= x1 < 4.7124` unknown x1-unit. Do not interpret all this as missing dynamics.

Important measurement finding: on every within-trajectory interior row, `dx1` equals the central finite difference `(x1_next-x1_previous)/(2*T_s)`, with `T_s = 0.01` s. RMSE of logged derivative minus this difference is 1.502801e-13 unknown dx1-unit on training interior rows and 1.481476e-13 unknown dx1-unit on validation interior rows. Thus `dx1` is a centered sampled derivative target, not independently observed instantaneous acceleration. Training minimum nonzero spacings are 0.00314159 unknown y-unit, 0.15707963 unknown x1-unit, and 7.85398163 unknown dx1-unit, consistent with quantization. Centered differentiation mixes neighboring input effects and warrants caution near input changes.
