# Identification record

## Data and validation policy

Only `data/train.csv` and `data/val.csv` were used. They remain separate trajectories throughout: 1452 training samples and 1454 validation samples, sampled every 5 s. The original brief and file schema were read first; the prospective family list is in `plan.md`.

The measured pressure `p` is approximately absolute pressure: training spans 0.992309–2.186718 bar and validation 0.994018–2.144479 bar. Training inlet `flow` spans 0.097345–30.796778 l/min, and validation 0.083062–29.071159 l/min. The training valve stroke `valve` spans 10.307583–92.411713 %, and validation 1.628053–94.448708 %. The fully open and fully closed endpoints are therefore model-based extrapolations, not independently measured endpoint coefficients.

The supplied derivative `dp` closely resembles the forward pressure difference: its RMSE against `diff(p)/5` is 0.000221 bar/s on training and 0.000336 bar/s on validation. This does not make it a trustworthy instantaneous vector-field measurement when inputs change inside sampling intervals. Final parameter estimation uses pressure transitions followed by free-run pressure errors, never derivative regression.

Structure checks first fitted training alone and simulated validation without corrections. Further fits used the first two thirds of each experiment and evaluated the last third from its first measured state. These tail simulations deliberately reset only at the beginning of each held-out tail, not at later samples. Exact cuts are implemented as `int(len(d)*2/3)` in `fixed_atmosphere.py`. Final coefficients were refitted on both entire experiments; their full-trajectory errors are in-sample diagnostics, not independent test estimates. No test data were available or accessed.

## What was actually tried

1. `fit_models.py` explored linear pressure relaxation, smoothed square-root discharge, a fitted positive pressure power, compressible nozzle discharge with choking, mixed viscous/inertial resistance, pressure-density-enhanced discharge, and a pressure-dependent effective storage coefficient. Valve maps included positive cubic Bernstein combinations, power opening and exponential opening. All were fitted by bounded nonlinear least squares, first on one-step RK4 pressure predictions and then on full free-run training pressure. `fit_results.json` records actual starts, bounds, final values and metrics.
2. `refine_models.py` reduced the valve map to two monotone basis terms after the third coefficient was effectively zero. It imposed a bounded atmospheric restoring slope for the smoothed root/nozzle models and explored a fitted atmospheric reference. `refined_results.json` contains training-only, blocked and pooled fits. Some reference-pressure estimates reached their bounds, revealing model/calibration confounding rather than a determined atmospheric pressure. These were not delivered.
3. `fixed_atmosphere.py` therefore fixes `patm = 1` bar, as specified approximately in the brief, and refits the three delivered nonlinear outlet laws. Fixing this value sacrifices a small amount of fit flexibility but gives the required equilibrium directly and prevents smoothing errors from being hidden in a fitted atmospheric offset. `fixed_results.json` records all three fitting stages, including the final pooled coefficients.
4. `diagnostics.py` additionally fitted Lasso polynomial derivative regressions with no intercept: an inlet term `flow` and monomials `z**i*v**j`, where `z = p-1` bar, `v = valve/100`, pressure degrees run from one through three, and valve degrees from zero through three. Features were normalized by their training root-mean-square values. Scikit-learn Lasso used penalty settings `alpha = 1e-6` bar/s, `alpha = 1e-5` bar/s, and `alpha = 1e-4` bar/s, no random sampling, 200000 maximum iterations, and stopping tolerance `tol = 1e-10`. The smallest penalty produced a convergence warning and is not claimed as a converged optimum. Several resulting polynomials had positive pressure feedback or adverse closure response on a physical-domain grid, despite acceptable derivative errors. None was defensible as the delivered long-horizon model. This was sparse-regression diagnosis, not genetic symbolic regression; neither gplearn nor PySINDy was used.
5. The helper's scripts `helper_inspect.py`, `helper_transition.py` and `helper_mixed.py` explored input alignment using train-fitted transition models. Pure current-input transition fits gave approximately 0.011034 bar training and 0.009968 bar validation one-step RMSE. Using current valve and next flow reduced these to 0.005955 bar and 0.006821 bar; jointly estimated input blending gave a next-flow weight of approximately 0.84653 and near-zero next-valve weight, with 0.005724 bar and 0.006739 bar errors. These are diagnostics of timing or omitted dynamics, not usable causal candidates. They do not prove a unique logging delay. Future samples were not used in any delivered expression or its final estimation.

## Selection and residual examination

| Mixed viscous/inertial | 0.038665 | 0.023717 | 0.057941 | 0.026168 | 0.037484 |
| Regularized Bernoulli | 0.039234 | 0.025531 | 0.055572 | 0.026510 | 0.037919 |
| Regularized choked nozzle | 0.039679 | 0.027055 | 0.055087 | 0.026860 | 0.038294 |

The provisional linear family had approximately 0.062466 bar training and 0.061803 bar validation free-run RMSE and large systematic pressure/flow residuals. It was useful as a diagnostic but was not retained for a regime where inertial discharge is clearly needed. A fitted pressure exponent was close to one half, so it did not warrant a separate coefficient-variant candidate. The density-enhanced approximation performed worse. Pressure-dependent storage gave only a minor improvement in an exploratory fit and was not retained as an independently defensible additional state-free mechanism.

Residuals were examined by pressure, stroke, inlet flow, consecutive time blocks, input increments and lag autocorrelation. Detailed tables for provisional models are in `diagnostics.txt`, and for the delivered JSON equations in `final_diagnostics.txt`. In the recommended final mixed model, transition residual correlations with next-minus-current flow increments are approximately -0.807 on training and -0.716 on validation. The consecutive sixth-trajectory validation free-run RMSE values are approximately 0.021087, 0.014623, 0.015023, 0.051232, 0.013816 and 0.068806 bar. Large late-trajectory residuals persist across nonlinear families, so replacing the outlet law cannot be claimed to resolve them. No arbitrary pressure-independent drift or outlet leakage term was added: such terms would undermine zero-flow atmospheric equilibrium or the sealed endpoint. The retained mixed resistance and the cusp-regularized outlet families are the targeted structural responses to low-pressure residuals and numerical stiffness.

## Verification and physical scope

`deliver_models.py` creates the three JSON candidates and `models/pool.json` from the final pooled fit results. `verify_models.py` evaluates the actual JSON expressions, rather than assuming the generation is correct. Maximum equation differences from the fitting implementation are below 6e-17 bar/s on both observed trajectories. The JSON free-run RMSE values agree with the fitting metrics.

Monotonicity checks used pressures from -0.1 to 5 bar, valve closures from 0 to 100 % in one-percentage-point increments, and nonnegative inlet flows up to 30 l/min. All flow and closure increments had the required derivative sign; atmospheric zero-flow derivatives were exactly zero; sealed-valve derivatives were nonnegative. These checks supplement the analytic properties described in each candidate file; they are not proof based on a finite grid alone.

For each candidate, zero-flow simulations used initial pressures 0.7, 1.0001 and 2.3 bar, strokes 0, 30 and 90 %, and one RK4 step per 5 s for 2000 s. All nine cases per candidate returned to 1 bar within floating-point accuracy. Numerical open-valve pressure restoring slopes on a 1–10 bar grid had maxima approximately 0.500000 1/s for mixed resistance, 0.406245 1/s for regularized Bernoulli and 0.379680 1/s for the nozzle model. The mixed resistance estimate reaches its lower allowed value `rvisc = 2` s: it is partly limited by numerical time resolution and should not be interpreted as a measured pipe resistance.

The valve-independent term `-back*Min(p-patm,0)`, with `back = 0.1` 1/s, is explicitly a numerical vacuum extension. It preserves the stated global monotonicity requirements but does not purport to describe real bidirectional valve flow or a perfectly sealed tank under vacuum. Above atmosphere, all terms have the intended inventory/discharge interpretation. The two smoothed candidates have material, fitted crossover pressures, not negligibly small numerical epsilons. Hardware coefficients, actual gas temperature, controller calibration, bubble holdup and hidden transport dynamics cannot be separated from these data.

## Reproduction and sources

Run `python fixed_atmosphere.py`, then `python deliver_models.py`, `python verify_models.py`, and `python check_submission.py`. The fitting scripts import `fit_models.py` and `refine_models.py` for the common vector field and parameter mapping. The environment supplied NumPy 2.5.3, SciPy 1.18.1 and scikit-learn 1.9.1; Numba 0.68.0 and llvmlite 0.50.0 were installed during the round to compile the simulation loops. All fitting is deterministic and uses no random seed.

The NASA Glenn Research Center source **Mass Flow Choking**, last updated May 13, 2021, was fetched and checked for the compressible nozzle mass-flow and sonic-limit framework. The official SciPy **least_squares** documentation was fetched and checked for the Trust Region Reflective method, Jacobian scheme, bound handling, scaling and stopping settings. No source text was copied into the model discussion. The empirical monotone valve map, the low-pressure crossover correction and the numerical vacuum extension were modeling choices made in this round, not claims taken from those references.
