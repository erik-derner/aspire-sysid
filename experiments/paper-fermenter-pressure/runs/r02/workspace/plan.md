# Identification plan, before fitting

Compare these families: (1) ideal-gas inventory balance with linear pressure relaxation and monotone valve conductance (laminar approximation); (2) ideal-gas inventory balance with signed square-root pressure-drop discharge (orifice approximation), testing power-law and equal-percentage valve conductance; (3) compressible, subcritical/choked nozzle discharge with monotone valve conductance; (4) sparse polynomial pressure/input regression as a diagnostic, retaining only physically defensible structures; (5) if residuals indicate a need, a mixed viscous/inertial resistance discharge law or a positive pressure-dependent storage coefficient. These are planned alternatives, not a statement that all will be delivered.

Inspect both observed trajectories, units and input excitation first. Use train.csv for parameter estimation and val.csv for independent structure selection; prefer pressure-transition or long-horizon simulation criteria over the filtered derivative alone, since evaluation integrates pressure without measurement corrections. Initially fit transitions, then optimize full free-run training pressure where numerically feasible. Simulate with exactly one classical RK4 step per 5 s and zero-order-held inputs. Examine derivative/transition and free-run residuals by pressure, valve, flow and input changes. Positive coefficients, monotone closure response and atmospheric equilibrium will be built into candidate structures. Keep structurally distinct, defensible candidates, and record actual starts, bounds, methods, metrics and limitations. No test data will be accessed.
