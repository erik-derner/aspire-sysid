FREE-RESPONSE REST AND CONSTRAINTS
Near-rest free-response subset (`Abs(omega)<0.01` rad/s and `Abs(domega)<0.01` rad/s^2): 1653 training samples; angle range = [-0.0879646, 0.163363] rad. A smooth Coulomb-only model predicts return toward exact equilibrium except at zeros of restoring torque; a static threshold can sustain this observed interval.
For existing families let `D` denote input acceleration minus restoring acceleration, `h = exp(-(omega/v)**2)` and `clip(D) = Min(Max(D,-fs),fs)`. The core `D-h*clip(D)` has derivative with respect to `D` between `1-h` and 1; hence increasing input maps imply R3 (nondecreasing, flat within a static band at rest). At `omega=0` and `u=0`, its sign equals the sign of `D` or it is zero, so R2 follows if restoring torque opposes angle. These arguments are analytic and do not rely on fitted-domain sampling.
Family 5 uses `-g*sin(theta)*(1+q*cos(theta))`; `g>=0` and `Abs(q)<=1` suffice for R2 on the stated angle domain. Family 6 changes friction to `-(c+d*tanh(omega/v))*tanh(omega/v)`; `c>=Abs(d)` keeps friction opposing velocity, and this term vanishes at rest so does not change R2. Family 4 input `a*u+k*u**3` has slope `a+3*k*u**2`; positive cubic coefficient enforces hardening only. A negative cubic coefficient is also R3-valid if `a+300*k>=0` rad/(s^2 V) for the task input range, with the numeric factor carrying V^2. Saturating monotone maps avoid this global restriction.
